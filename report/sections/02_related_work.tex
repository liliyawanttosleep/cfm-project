\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
For each line of work we state what it contributes and how we use it.

\paragraph{Diffusion models.} \citet{song2019ncsn} learn the score $\nabla\log p$ of data perturbed at many noise levels and sample with annealed Langevin dynamics. DDPM \citep{ho2020ddpm} derives its objective from a variational bound on a 1,000-step noising chain and reduces it to a regression on the added noise ($\epsilon$-prediction); we use its linear schedule and loss for our diffusion reference. \citet{song2021sde} view both as discretised stochastic differential equations and show that a deterministic \emph{probability-flow ODE} has the same marginals, which is what makes diffusion comparable with CFM. DDIM \citep{song2021ddim} integrates this ODE on a coarse grid ($\eta=0$) and gives usable samples in 10--100 steps; it is our diffusion sampler. EDM \citep{karras2022edm} separates the design choices of diffusion samplers and reaches FID 1.97 on CIFAR-10 with a second-order Heun solver on a tuned grid at 35 NFE, which motivates our solver-and-grid study. \citet{luo2022understanding} derives the variational, score and noise views in one notation; we used it to check Section~\ref{sec:diffusion}.

\paragraph{Flow matching.} \citet{lipman2023fm} regress a network on a velocity field that transports noise to data and show that conditioning on a data point gives a tractable loss with the same gradient; \citet{lipman2024guide} is a later guide with code. Rectified flow \citep{liu2023rf} uses straight paths between independent pairs (our I-CFM) and introduces \emph{reflow}, retraining on the model's own noise--sample pairs, with theorems on marginals and transport cost; we use reflow and its straightness measure. Stochastic interpolants \citep{albergo2023si} generalise such interpolations and identify the velocity as a conditional expectation. Neural ODEs \citep{chen2018node} made adaptive ODE solvers usable in deep learning; we use their Dormand--Prince solver \citep{dormand1980rk} as the accurate reference. OT-CFM \citep{tong2024otcfm} pairs each minibatch by exact optimal transport and reports straighter paths; its \texttt{torchcfm} library provides our models and U-Net. Stable Diffusion~3 \citep{esser2024sd3} trains rectified flows at scale with logit-normal training times and a grid shifted towards noise; we test the first and use the second as a family of grids.

\paragraph{Few-step generation.} Consistency models \citep{song2023cm} learn a map from any point of a trajectory to its end (one-step FID 3.55 on CIFAR-10 by distillation), and MeanFlow \citep{geng2025meanflow} learns the average velocity over an interval. Both change the training objective and fall outside our fixed-regression comparison.

\paragraph{Transport, numerics and evaluation.} \citet{villani2009ot} gives the uniqueness of solutions of the continuity equation (Proposition~\ref{prop:transport}); \citet{gelbrich1990} bounds a transport cost from below by that of Gaussians with the same moments, which yields our pairing bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:bound}); POT \citep{flamary2021pot} provides the exact OT solver. \citet{hairer1993ode} is our reference for local errors of Euler, Heun and Runge--Kutta methods and for Gr\"onwall-type error propagation. FID \citep{heusel2017fid} compares Gaussian fits to Inception-v3 features \citep{szegedy2016inception}; it is biased upward roughly as $1/n$ \citep{chong2020fid} and sensitive to image resizing, which clean-fid avoids \citep{parmar2022cleanfid}. Our class-coverage test combines $k$-means \citep{lloyd1982kmeans} with Pearson's $\chi^2$ test of homogeneity \citep{agresti2013cda}, Cohen's effect size \citep{cohen1988power} and the bootstrap \citep{efron1979bootstrap}; Tweedie's formula \citep{efron2011tweedie} links the score to the posterior mean of the noise.
