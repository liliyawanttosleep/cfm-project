Method II · Flow Matching

Conditional flow matching: regress the velocity

Ideal objective: marginal field uₜ(x) is unknown

L_FM(θ) = E ‖ vθ(x, t) − uₜ(x) ‖²

Condition on a pair (x₀, x₁): a straight line

xₜ = (1 − t) x₀ + t x₁, uₜ(x | x₀, x₁) = x₁ − x₀

Tractable objective

L_CFM(θ) = E ‖ vθ(xₜ, t) − (x₁ − x₀) ‖²

Theorem (Lipman et al., 2023)

∇θ L_CFM = ∇θ L_FM

Why: uₜ(x) = E[ x₁ − x₀ | xₜ = x ]. Least squares on a noisy target still recovers the conditional mean — the regression function.

Sampling: Euler steps x ← x + vθ(x, t)/N, so NFE = N.

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 9

[Wentao · ~2.5 min] The ideal objective is to regress the network onto the marginal velocity field u_t(x) that carries noise to data. But we cannot compute u_t(x). The trick: draw a pair, a noise point x_0 and a data point x_1, and move along the straight line between them. Along that line the velocity is simply x_1 minus x_0. So we regress the network at x_t onto x_1 minus x_0. Lipman et al. prove that this conditional loss has exactly the same gradient as the ideal one. The statistical reason is clean: the marginal velocity is the conditional expectation of x_1 minus x_0 given x_t. Least squares on a noisy target recovers the conditional mean — the regression function. So flow matching is a regression problem, just like DDPM's noise regression. To sample we integrate the ODE with Euler steps; N steps means NFE = N.
