Method I · Diffusion

DDPM: add noise, learn to predict it

Forward process (fixed, no parameters)

q(xₜ | x₀) = N( √ᾱₜ x₀, (1 − ᾱₜ) I )

xₜ = √ᾱₜ x₀ + √(1 − ᾱₜ) ε, ε ~ N(0, I), ᾱₜ = ∏ₛ≤ₜ (1 − βₛ)

Training objective (Ho et al., 2020)

L(θ) = E t, x₀, ε ‖ ε − εθ(xₜ, t) ‖²

In statistical terms

A simple least-squares regression: input a noisy point and its noise level, output the noise.

The full ELBO reduces to this weighted MSE.

Convention: DDPM puts data at t = 0 and noise at t = T = 1000, the reverse of flow matching.

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 6

[Kaiming · ~1.5 min] DDPM's forward process adds Gaussian noise step by step. It has no parameters, and at any time t the noisy point has a closed form: x_t equals root alpha-bar times x_0 plus root one-minus-alpha-bar times epsilon. Training draws a data point, a time and a noise vector, builds x_t, and asks the network to recover the noise with a squared error. Ho et al. showed that the variational bound reduces to this weighted MSE, and in practice the unweighted version works best. So statistically this is just a least-squares regression. One notation warning: DDPM puts data at t = 0 and noise at t = T, the opposite of flow matching.
