Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 22 · W2 to a held-out test set, 2,000 samples, seed 0; lower is better

[Kaiming · ~1 min] The same comparison quantitatively: W2 against the number of steps for all four datasets, one seed. The ordering is the same everywhere and at every budget: OT-CFM, then I-CFM, then DDIM. OT-CFM is flat from the very first step. I-CFM needs 20 to 50 steps to come close on three datasets, and on eight Gaussians it levels off at 0.31, well above OT-CFM's 0.20. DDIM fails at one and two steps, with W2 between 7 and 13, and is still the worst at 100 steps. On the swiss roll OT-CFM sits at the floor; the floor itself is estimated from 2,000 samples, so differences of about 0.01 are noise. The table reads off the five-step column: OT-CFM is already below DDPM with 1,000 steps on all four datasets. This is hypothesis H1 in 2D; the open question is whether it survives in high dimensions.
