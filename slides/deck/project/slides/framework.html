Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 5

[Wentao · ~1.5 min] Both families share one picture. Start from a standard Gaussian at t = 0 and move along a probability path p_t until we reach the data distribution at t = 1. The animation shows two such paths on the two-moons data: on the left flow matching's straight interpolation, on the right diffusion's noising path, written in the same direction. (The PDF shows still snapshots instead of the animation.) The network learns a time-dependent vector field v(x, t), and the continuity equation tells us how the density moves with it. To generate, we solve the ODE. The number of network calls the solver makes is the NFE — this is our unit of sampling cost, and it is the x-axis of every comparison later.
