Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 24

[Kaiming · ~1.5 min] From theory and the literature we state four hypotheses, each with a test. H1, few-step advantage: at ten steps or fewer, OT-CFM beats I-CFM, which beats DDIM, with stochastic DDPM far behind. Our 2D results agree. H2, convergence: with 100 or more steps the gaps shrink, because all methods transport between the same two marginals. H3, the most statistical one: the OT advantage fades with dimension. With a batch much smaller than the dimension, empirical optimal-transport pairs within a minibatch look almost random. In 2D the one-step W2 of OT-CFM and I-CFM differs by a factor of 7 to 11 across our four datasets; on CIFAR-10 their FIDs are 3.58 versus 3.66. We will measure this gap in 2D, on MNIST and on CIFAR-10. H4: utility tracks quality — lower FID should go with higher TSTR accuracy, a discriminator AUC closer to 0.5 and more uniform cluster shares.
