Experimental design

Metrics: distances between distributions

Wasserstein-2 (2D)

W₂² = min over π ∈ Π(μ, ν) of E_π ‖x − y‖²

Exact optimal transport between samples (POT)

MMD (2D)

MMD² = E k(x, x′) + E k(y, y′) − 2 E k(x, y)

Unbiased U-statistic, multi-bandwidth RBF kernel

FID (images)

‖μᵣ − μ_g‖² + Tr(Σᵣ + Σ_g − 2(Σᵣ Σ_g)^½)

= W₂² between Gaussians fitted to Inception features

Straightness (paths)

S = mean over steps of ‖(x_N − x₀) − Δx/Δt‖²

0 = perfectly straight trajectory

Caveat: Inception features suit natural images, not digits, so on MNIST we also compute FID on features from our own MNIST classifier.

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 16

[Kaiming · ~1.5 min] Every metric is a distance between two distributions. W2: exact optimal transport between the two samples in 2D, computed with the POT library. MMD: the kernel two-sample statistic, unbiased, with several RBF bandwidths. FID, the standard image metric. A point for a statistics audience: FID fits a Gaussian to the Inception features of each set and takes the closed-form W2 distance between the two Gaussians. Straightness measures how close the sampling trajectory is to a straight line; zero means perfectly straight. Caveat: Inception was trained on natural images, not handwritten digits, so on MNIST we also compute FID on features from our own MNIST classifier.
