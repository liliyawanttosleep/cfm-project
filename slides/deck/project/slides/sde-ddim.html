SDE view and DDIM: the same model, fewer steps

Score SDE (Song et al., 2021)

VP-SDE (continuous-time DDPM)

dx = −½ β(t) x dt + √β(t) dW

Probability-flow ODE: same marginals pₜ, no noise

dx = [ −½ β(t) x − ½ β(t) ∇ₓ log pₜ(x) ] dt

DDIM, η = 0 (Song, Meng & Ermon, 2021)

x̂₀ = ( xₜ − √(1 − ᾱₜ) εθ ) / √ᾱₜ

xₛ = √ᾱₛ x̂₀ + √(1 − ᾱₛ) εθ, s < t

A discretization of the ODE: deterministic, and steps can be skipped. On CIFAR-10, FID 13.36 at 10 steps and 4.16 at 100.

Watch out: x̂₀ divides by √ᾱₜ ≈ 0.006 at t = T, so with 1–2 steps the error explodes (see our 2D demo).

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 8 · CIFAR-10 numbers: Song et al. (2021), DDIM Table 1

[Kaiming · ~2 min] Song et al. wrote DDPM as a variance-preserving SDE. The key fact: there is a deterministic probability-flow ODE whose marginals p_t match the SDE at every time, and the score it needs comes from our trained noise network. DDIM with eta = 0 is a discretization of that ODE. First estimate x_0 from the current noise prediction, then jump to an earlier time s. No retraining is needed, and the same model can sample in 10 to 100 steps; on CIFAR-10 the DDIM paper reports FID 13.36 at 10 steps and 4.16 at 100. One detail matters for our demo: at t = T, root alpha-bar is about 0.006, and the x_0 estimate divides by it, so with only one or two steps the error is amplified roughly 150 times. This slide is the bridge: diffusion, too, can be read as learning a vector field and solving an ODE.
