Part I · Generation as transport

Velocity moves density: the continuity equation

∂ₜ pₜ(x) + ∇ · ( pₜ(x) uₜ(x) ) = 0

pₜ(x) uₜ(x) is the flux: density times velocity.

In words

Probability mass is conserved. Density at x rises when more mass flows in than flows out.

Why it helps generation

It is our test: if a (pₜ, uₜ) pair satisfies it with p₁ = p_data, integrating uₜ from p₀ gives data.

In code

Never computed. We use it only to prove that the regression target is correct.

[Kaiming · ~1.5 min] A velocity field moves individual points, but we care about the whole distribution. The continuity equation connects the two. The term p times u is the flux, how much probability crosses a location per unit time. The equation says that probability mass is conserved, as in fluid flow: the density at x changes only because mass flows in or out. For example, look at the arrows at t equals 0.75: they point toward the two peaks from both sides, so mass piles up there and the peaks grow. Why does this matter for generation? It gives us a test for correctness. Suppose we have a probability path p_t that starts at the noise distribution and ends at the data distribution, and a velocity field u_t that satisfies this equation with it. Then integrating u_t from noise gives samples from the data distribution. We never compute this equation in code. We use it once, later, to prove that our regression target is the right one.
