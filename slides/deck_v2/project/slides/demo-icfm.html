[Wentao · ~1 min] Now we train on real 2D data, using the experiment from the torchcfm tutorial notebook. The source is eight Gaussians on a circle, the target is two moons, and the network is a small MLP. Training uses exactly the five lines we just labelled. Three things to notice. First, it works: samples reach the moons, although the network only saw straight-line targets. Second, the source is not a standard Gaussian; we only had to sample from it. Third, look at the dark paths. They wander, and some loop back. On average, a path is 35 percent longer than the straight line from its start to its end. That is strange, since every training target was straight. It also matters, because a solver must follow the curve, and each step costs one network evaluation. So why do the paths bend?
