Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 19 · Our runs: source N(0, I), standardized data, 2,000 samples, seed 0. Not comparable with slide 18.

[Wentao · ~1.3 min] This is our own experiment, on four datasets, with a fixed number of solver steps. The source is now a standard Gaussian and the data are standardized, so do not compare with the last table. Each curve is one run, seed 0. Lower is better, and the dashed line is the distance between two sets of real samples. OT-CFM, orange circles, is nearly flat from the first step, so in 2D one to five steps are enough. I-CFM, brown squares, starts above 1, as we predicted, and comes close only at 20 to 50 steps. On 8 Gaussians, it stays at 0.31, above OT-CFM at 0.20. DDIM, blue triangles, is a fast sampler for diffusion models, and it is worst at few steps. The diamond is DDPM with 1,000 steps. The pictures show moons at 5 steps: both flow models keep the shape, and DDIM does not. Kaiming explains why next. Rectified flow is another way to straighten: train, let the model generate its own pairs, and retrain on them. Details are in the backup. This answers question 3. Kaiming, over to you.
