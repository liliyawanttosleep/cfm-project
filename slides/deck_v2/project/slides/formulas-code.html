Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 14 · Simplified: t must be reshaped to match x. In torchcfm, sample_location_and_conditional_flow does rows 2–4.

[Wentao · ~1.3 min] Now we can go back to our five lines and label each of them. Draw a pair: that is z. Draw a time uniformly. Compute the point on the line: this is a sample from the path p_t. Compute the target: x₁ minus x₀. Regress with mean squared error: this is the conditional flow matching loss. In the torchcfm library, one function does the time, the point and the target together, and that is the long function name we saw earlier. Generation is the Euler loop from Kaiming’s part: start from noise, and add the network’s velocity divided by K, K times. The chain at the bottom is the whole argument. We regress on a random target. By the variance decomposition, the network learns its posterior mean. By linearity, the posterior mean satisfies the continuity equation with our path. By the continuity equation, integrating it from noise generates data. This answers our first two questions. The third question remains: if this already works, why would we change anything? The answer is cost. Next, we see what happens with real 2D data and few steps.
