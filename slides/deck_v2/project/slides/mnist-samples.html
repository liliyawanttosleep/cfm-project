Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 25 · Every panel starts from the same 16 noise images. Bottom strip: OT-CFM. One-step numbers: 256 samples.

[Kaiming · ~1.3 min] Each cell starts from the same noise. Rows are methods, columns are network calls. The bottom strip shows generation over time with OT-CFM: digits appear by about t equals 0.5. Look at the first column. I-CFM gives the same gray blur in every cell. Compare it with the data mean, top right: it is the same picture. That is the prediction from slide 16, now at 784 dimensions. The numbers agree: after one step, I-CFM is 0.04 from the mean, while a real digit is 0.52 away. OT-CFM at one call is different. It is blurry, but you can already read digits, and different noise gives different digits. DDIM at one or two calls gives white specks. About three quarters of the pixels leave the valid range, with values up to 19. That is the division by a small a from slide 22. A second call adds little, because it comes at the last grid step. By 10 calls all three look like digits, and pictures stop being a fine enough test. So Wentao will now measure this.
