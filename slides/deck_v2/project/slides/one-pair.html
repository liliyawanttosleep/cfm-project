[Wentao · ~1.2 min] Thanks, Kaiming. We now build the target in six steps, shown in the bar at the top. In each step we keep the same small example: noise from a standard Gaussian, and data at the two points plus and minus two. Step one is the easiest possible case: forget the distribution and take a single pair, one noise point x₀ and one data point x₁. Throughout this talk, t equals 0 is noise and t equals 1 is data. Connect the two points with a straight line traveled at constant speed. The position at time t is the linear interpolation, and differentiating with respect to t gives the velocity, x₁ minus x₀. It does not depend on t. In the figure, x₀ is minus 1.8 and x₁ is 2, so the velocity is 3.8 at every time. This is the target in our five lines of code. A note on the footer: the general formula for a Gaussian path has an extra term, but with constant sigma that term vanishes, which is why torchcfm simply returns x1 minus x0. One line reaches only one data point, so this is not yet a generative model. The next step is to use many pairs.
