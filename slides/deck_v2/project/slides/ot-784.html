Loss is not a quality score

OT-CFM’s final loss is lower, 0.118 vs. 0.168, partly because the floor changes with the pairing (B2).

Answer: left only at very few steps

On both seeds OT-CFM’s FID is 33–42% lower at 1–2 steps. At 5 steps: 41% vs. 3%. From 10 steps on, the seeds disagree.

Why weaker: only one term can change

E‖x₀ − x₁‖² = E‖x₀‖² + E‖x₁‖² − 2E⟨x₀, x₁⟩

No pairing can save more than 2 tr Σ^(1/2) = 386. A batch of 128 saves 66, and a batch of 2,048 saves 88. Pairs stay closer to random than to exact OT.

Our hypothesis: largely supported

We expected near-random batch pairs to erase the advantage. With two seeds it is gone from 5–10 steps on, but not at 1–2 steps.

Path length hides this

Most of each path’s length, about 39, only removes noise, so a ratio of 1.02 can still hide the 14.5 from mean to digit.

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 27 · W2 in 2D, FID on MNIST: compare within a column. Batch 128; pairs, paths: seed 0. Bound: Gelbrich 1990.

[Wentao · ~1.2 min] W2 in 2D and FID on MNIST are different scales, so read down each column, not across. In 2D, OT pairing changes almost everything: the cost falls by about 90 percent. On MNIST the ratio looks tiny, 0.96. But only the inner product can change. The most any pairing can save is 386, a bound from the covariance. A batch of 128 saves 66, which is 17 percent of that. Even a batch of 2,048 saves only 88. So minibatch pairs stay closer to random than to exact OT. We expected that to erase the advantage, and with two seeds it nearly does. At one and two steps OT-CFM is still 33 to 42 percent lower on both seeds. At five steps the gap is 41 percent on seed 0 but only 3 on seed 1, and from ten steps on the seeds disagree. Path length hides this: 1.02 looks straight, because most of the length just removes noise. And the lower loss is not a quality score, since the floor moves with the pairing. So our hypothesis is largely supported. FID is one score, though. Kaiming will now ask a different question with a course method.
