Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 17 · Minibatch OT only approximates π*. Lipman’s “OT path” is another idea: the path within one given pair.

[Wentao · ~1.6 min] If crossings cause the bending, let us stop them. Did Part II use that x₀ and x₁ are independent? It did not. The pair can follow any joint distribution with marginals p₀ and p₁. So the pairing is a free choice. Which pairing avoids crossings? Optimal transport: the coupling with the smallest expected squared length. Here is why. Take two lines crossing at time t, with velocities v and v prime, and swap their endpoints. The total squared length falls by exactly two t, one minus t, times the squared velocity difference. That is positive, so an optimal coupling cannot contain a crossing. Check it at our point. The crossing lines have squared lengths 3.8 squared and 4.2 squared, a total of 32.08. After the swap, each line moves only 0.2, a total of 0.08. The saving is 32, and the formula gives one half times 64, also 32. Below, a batch of 16 points shows the same: mean squared length falls from 41 to 9. In code, it is one more line: solve the assignment on each batch, then continue as before. Two warnings. This is minibatch OT, so it only approximates the true coupling. And Lipman’s “OT path” is different: it describes the path within one given pair. Here, OT decides who is paired with whom.
