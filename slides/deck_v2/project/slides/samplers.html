With a = t, b = 1 − t: x′ = x + Δt (x̂₁ − ε̂), exactly Euler. On VP, it is the same Euler step in rescaled x and t.

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 22 · W2 over 4 datasets, one network per method, seed 0. DDIM grid: equal DDPM steps; other grids not tested.

[Kaiming · ~1.4 min] One DDIM step uses the noise network to estimate both ends, then re-mixes them at the next time. On the straight path, this is exactly one Euler step. On VP, divide x by a plus b and use a over a plus b as the clock, and it is the same Euler step. In the figure, the orange VP paths fall on the straight ones. DDPM keeps the same marginals and adds noise: in the continuity equation, the extra drift and the noise cancel. So the gap on slide 19 is not about path shape. At one or two steps, the cause is dividing by a, which is 0.0064 at the first call. A noise error of 0.05 to 0.09 becomes 8 to 14, close to the measured W2. With the exact field on our example, one DDIM step gives 1.98, with no blow-up. At five steps, it is mostly the grid: the same velocity network goes from about 0.3 to about 0.5 on DDIM’s grid. This depends on the data: on our two-point example, DDIM’s grid does better. We have not separated what remains at 20 steps. Only the pairing changes the path. Next, images.
