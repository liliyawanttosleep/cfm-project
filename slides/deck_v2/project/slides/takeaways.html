Takeaways

1

Generation becomes regression.

Least squares recovers a posterior mean, and that posterior mean is a correct velocity field.

2

Pairing sets the path, and the path sets the steps.

In 2D, OT-CFM is nearly flat from one step, while I-CFM’s one step lands on the data mean.

3

Velocity, noise and score are one quantity.

They are linear conversions of each other. Few-step gaps come from those conversions and the time grid, not from path shape.

4

At 784 dimensions, the OT advantage is left only at very few steps.

On both seeds FID is 33–42% lower at 1–2 steps; from 10 steps on the seeds disagree, since batch pairs stay close to random.

5

Check more than FID, and more than one seed.

k-means found the lowest-FID model the most unbalanced; its second training run was balanced.

Flow Matching vs. Diffusion · 30 · MNIST: two training seeds per method.

[Wentao · ~0.8 min] Five things to remember. First, flow matching turns generation into regression: least squares gives a posterior mean, and that is a correct velocity field. Second, pairing sets the path, and the path sets the number of steps. Third, velocity, noise and score are one quantity. The few-step gaps between samplers come from conversions and grids, not from path shape. Fourth, in 784 dimensions the OT advantage is left only where steps are very few: about 33 to 42 percent lower FID at one and two steps, on both seeds. Fifth, check more than FID and more than one seed. The course method found that our best-FID model was the most unbalanced, and a second training run did not repeat it.
